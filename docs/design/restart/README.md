## Summary

Decision: The cockpit stays one screen, now in calm colour: a graphite tower with white edges, grey dimension lines and white numbers. Green marks only the picked part (with a soft halo), the picked row, the live dot and the primary button. A Build tab sets identical cells, the structure (FRP, steel, concrete) and a fill layer stack you reorder and size in module steps. A parts tray beside the tower pins parts by dragging them on, and refuses in plain words a part that can't work. Every edit re-rates live, can be undone, and numbers tween. Up to 3 designs sit side by side in a compare tray. Scenarios are saved by name and shared by link.
Screens: the cockpit at 1440 and 390 in eleven states: 3D, GA views, refused duty, light, first paint, fill layers, 3 cells (3D and GA), structure check, a part pinned and one refused, and compare. Plus the phone sheets.
The structure panel has a load-code picker: Thai DPT (1311 wind, 1301 seismic) by default, or ASCE 7, each with its own site inputs. The ranking sorts by the rank-by key, marked on its column. Panels carry no helper prose lines.
Open questions: (1) Saved scenarios live in the browser and a link carries the whole scenario, so nothing is stored server-side and there is no spend. Is that enough, or do you want accounts? (2) Still open from last round: three.js loads right after first paint, behind the poster, not on scroll. OK?

## What impl takes from here

- Colour is structural. `tokens.css` adds `--edge`, `--dim`, `--num`, `--model-fill`, `--model-fill-2`, `--model-fill-3` and `--model-concrete`. `--accent` is used in four places only: the picked part and its halo, the picked ranking row, the live dot and `.btn.pri`. The halo is the picked part's edges on their own layer, blurred under the edge canvas. No other glow.
- `model.js` builds every cell from `cells[]` at its own `origin` (shapes.md: cells along +x). Fill layers come from `fill[]`, top first, each drawn as a band by its fill kind. `structure` picks the casing (FRP seams, steel ribs and bracing, concrete walls and lintels). `pick` and `refuse` mark a part by name (`fan`, `fill:2`, `frame`). Anchors give the screen point for callouts, pin badges and drop targets.
- `fixture.js` derives the cell geometry from a few inputs (basin, inlet, layers, fan), the same way the engine will (#2 cells, #3 structure, #4 pinning). The default duty's numbers are still the recorded `select` step. The layer, cell, structure and pin numbers are layout figures until those issues land.
- Edits are one stream: each edit (duty, layer, cell count, structure, pin) pushes onto the undo stack, rates at once in the rating worker, and re-ranks in the sizing worker (ADR: rating never waits behind sizing). The ranking shows skeleton rows while it re-ranks. Numbers tween about 250 ms to their new value and show the delta briefly.
- Scenarios: named, kept in the browser. A share link carries the whole scenario in the URL and opens read-only.
- `./render.sh` renders every state (or one: `./render.sh layers`) with Playwright's cached headless Chromium. It loads three.js from `web/vendor/`.
